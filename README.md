# Brick Royale: Memewars (BrickBrawl 1v1)

Plain HTML + CSS + JavaScript, no build step. Open `index.html` in a desktop browser (it loads three.js r128 from a CDN, so it needs internet),
or run `python3 -m http.server` in this folder. It also works as-is on GitHub Pages: push this folder and enable Pages.

Controls: WASD move, Space jump, mouse aim, left click shoot (or swing the hammer), hold right click to aim (scope on the sniper), F quick hammer, Q ability, T sprint, Shift crouch, V first/third person,
1 gun (press again to swap primary/secondary, also X or mouse wheel), 2 hammer, 3 4 5 use loot items, R reload, M mute.

## Match rules
- Health scales with level: a fighter's max health follows how much damage a gun of their level does (level 1 is about 35% of level 30), so low-level fights are as short as high-level ones. Armor, heals, hammer, grenades, traps and airstrikes are scaled by the same number.
- Round clock: at 1:30 no more health, shield or loot-bag spawns and a red circular zone closes toward the middle (it is only a few blocks wide at 2:30). The zone is one flat see-through red; standing outside it burns health and shows a big OUT OF THE ZONE banner with an arrow to the middle, a pulsing red screen edge and a flashing health bar. Loot is scarce: 4 loot bags and 4 health/shield pickups, each with a long respawn (60-90 s). At 2:30 with nobody down, the round goes to the player with MORE health (armor, then the host, breaks ties). Tuning numbers are at the top of `js/storm.js`.
- Port-a-Fortress (loot item): throw it and a brick tower with a bounce pad at its base appears where it lands. Step on the pad to get launched onto the roof. `js/fort.js`.

## Online play (ranked + parties), all on Supabase
Matchmaking, ratings, party codes AND the match itself run on your Supabase project. Players do not connect to each other: both join a private
Realtime broadcast channel and updates are relayed through Supabase (batched ~10 times a second), so it works on any network.
1. Supabase > SQL Editor > New query: paste `supabase/setup.sql` and Run (safe to run again).
2. Authentication > Sign In / Providers: turn on **Allow anonymous sign-ins**.
3. Realtime: make sure Realtime is enabled for the project and **Allow public access** is on (Realtime > Settings). This is the default.
4. `js/config.js` holds the project URL and publishable key (already filled in; the publishable key is meant to be public).
5. Host the site over https (GitHub Pages is fine) and test in two different browsers or one normal plus one private window.

Ranked: Elo, start 1000, tiers Bronze / Silver 900 / Gold 1100 / Platinum 1300 / Diamond 1500 / Legend 1700. The server only changes ratings when both
players report matching results.

Capacity: Supabase counts every message sent and every message delivered. One match uses about 25-50 per second, so the Free plan (100/s) fits
about 2 matches at once and Pro (500/s) about 10-15. Raise `NET_MS` in `js/config.js` to use less (choppier) or lower it for smoother play.
Other limits: players are anonymous accounts (win-trading with extra accounts is possible) and ping depends on how far players are from the Supabase region.


## Rules worth knowing
- **Health is private.** Your own bar floats over your head (only you see it). The opponent's bar only appears over them for a few seconds after you hit them, and their health is not shown in the top corner.
- **Names are filtered** (`js/profile.js`): swear words, slurs and hateful or identity-based combinations are rejected, including look-alike spellings (leet speak, spacing, repeated letters). Edit the word lists there if you need to add or allow something.
- **Round clock** is a draining bar with the countdown written inside it (`js/storm.js`).
- **Weapon swap**: the gun you are not holding hangs on your back; swapping holsters it and draws the other with the swap animation (`js/hold.js`, `js/loadout.js`). Every gun is balanced to roughly the same sustained damage at its intended range.

- **Combat notes:** headshots (top 28% of the body) do 1.75x damage; the shotguns were buffed (pump 12.5 x 7, tactical 6.8 x 6) because they only work up close; burst-rifle ammo is shown as real bullets (bursts x 3).

## Exact aiming
- Every shot is solved by `aimSolve()` in `js/game.js` (`window.AIMSOLVE`): a ray from the camera through the crosshair finds the point under it (floor, walls, or the enemy's hit cylinder). The bullet starts at the gun (the raised gun's real muzzle when aimed in, via `BV.muzzle`) and flies a straight line through that point. If cover right at the gun would stop it short (peeking round a corner), the bullet starts on the crosshair ray itself, just ahead of your body.
- Bullet-vs-fighter hit tests use the exact entry point of the hit cylinder (`cylS`), so the impact lands precisely where aimed.
- Fully aimed in = zero random spread (`js/bullets.js`); shotgun pellets keep their pattern centred on the crosshair. Burst rounds re-solve each round. The gun barrel (`js/hold.js`) points along `P.ad`, the solved shot direction.

## Smooth gun and camera while aiming and moving
- `js/hold.js`: the claw/forearm twist chases its target at a limited speed (it used to snap half a turn when the choice flipped mid-stride); the barrel eases toward the shot line; recoil builds over a couple of frames; the support hand finds its reachable spot by scanning the barrel (the old bisection could jump on pistols).
- `js/game.js`: the third-person camera finds the room behind you smoothly (`camBack`) and eases in/out (`camState`, max 16 m/s in / 6 m/s out) so it no longer pops next to walls. The gun model's barrel (`P.ad`, from `aimSolve().vis`) never strays more than ~3.5 degrees from the camera direction; the bullets themselves still use the exact line (`aimSolve().dir`).
- Test: a scripted run of every gun while strafing, sprinting, crouching, jumping and firing checks per-frame arm turns, barrel turns, gun and camera jumps, and that aim stays fully raised.

## Random pickups, solid roofs, first person
- `js/spawns.js`: health, armor and loot bags are never at fixed spots. Two health and two armor (one each in opposite quarters of the arena, which diagonal is picked every round) plus ONE loot bag (middle strip, there from the start of every round, 45 s to come back). Each pickup that is collected comes back at a fresh spot in the same area. Spots are clear of walls and crates and never on a fighter's start. Placement is a hash of (match seed, round, pickup, respawn count), not a running random generator, so both online screens agree. Counts are `PK.counts()`; to change them edit the `chests` list in `game.js` / `spots` in `arsenal.js`.
- Roofs (`js/town.js`, `js/game.js`): every roof layer is a real solid with an underside (`y0`) and a top (`h`). You can land on a roof from above (e.g. off a Port-a-Fortress), walk up its 0.5 steps (`STEP`) and stand on the ridge; you cannot walk or jump through it, you bump your head on it indoors and on door lintels (`HEAD`), and the chimney is solid. `gh(x,z,y)` is the standing height at a spot for feet at height y.
- First person (`js/fpv.js`): press V (remembered). The camera sits at your eyes and your head, torso and legs are hidden so a roof or wall can never block the view; arms and gun stay, held up in front of you (`FPG` in `hold.js`). Death returns to third person.

## Melee weapons (round 7, reworked in round 8)
Fifteen melee weapons (six common-pool ones anyone can pick, nine signature ones that belong to one fighter each), picked on their own third menu page, **Choose your melee** (menu flow: fighter, weapon, melee, then Fight). The page shows the equipped weapon big on top with every stat and its effect, then one section per rarity from Common up to Legendary (within a rarity, weaker to stronger by damage per second). Built by `buildMelee` in `js/ui.js`, styled at the end of `css/style.css` (saved in the browser, shown in hotbar slot 2, used with F / click when the melee slot is out).
All of it lives in `js/melee.js`: models, stats, grips, swing animations, hit effects, picker icons.

**Holding and swinging.** The weapon sits in a pivot at the fist and its lean is solved in world terms, so bats, hammers, maces and clubs are held upright (head up) and the katana is held forward-up in a guard. The sword and bat style hold is `h:{gy,sc,phi,...}` in each `LIST` entry (`gy` = where on the handle the fist sits, `sc` = size in hand, `phi` = lean from straight up). Three swing styles in `STY`: **chop** (overhead smash: hammer, ban hammer, mace, wrench, brain bonker, Sahur bat), **slash** (sideways sweep: brick bat, paddle) and **diag** (diagonal cut: katana, bone club). Every swing is right-handed and cuts ACROSS the body: wound up behind the right shoulder with the torso turned right, then hips and shoulders unwind to the left, the weapon passes the target and carries on across to the left side (follow-through) before coming back to guard. The motion is a smooth curve through the key poses (`trk`), so speed builds into the impact and carries through it. The torso twists, the legs counter-twist, weight goes onto the front foot, and the free arm counter-swings. (Fixed: the free arm used to wind up and twist during swings because its angle was added to every frame instead of set.) Drawing the weapon (key 2) brings it up from the hip. In first person it leans further forward so it does not block the view.

**Swing animations.** Hammers, maces and the wrench use `smash`: the weapon is raised behind the head, the body leans back, then it comes down in a steep arc with a forward bow of the torso and the head ends pointing at the ground. Swords (katana, sabre, bone club) use `diag`: a forehand cut from the upper right down across the body to the lower left, and the next swing is a backhand that comes back across (`diag2`, alternated by `e._alt`). Bats and paddles keep the sideways slash, the Sahur bat and Brain Bonker the chop. The torso bow is `e._bx`, applied in `walkAnim` on top of the running lean.

**Hits are decided by contact** (`qstep` / `blade` / `touch` in `js/melee.js`, `meleeHit` in game.js): during the fast part of a swing (24%-86% of it) nine points along the weapon, from the fist to the far end of the head, are tested against the other fighter's body (an upright cylinder, radius 0.5, feet to head; the head and handle have their own thickness, measured from the model). The weapon's path since the previous frame is swept in four steps so a fast swing cannot skip through someone. The first touch deals the damage, once per swing; nothing lands unless the weapon really reaches them, and anything it visibly touches is hit. A wall between the swinger and the contact point blocks the blow, and a stunned or dead swinger loses it. Online, the victim's game does the same on its own screen. ## Battle Royale: 3x bigger map, drop zone, minimap, drops (round 26)
- **The arena is three times as big in each direction (180 x 180 m).** `MAPS.applyTiled(id,3)` (`js/maplib.js`) builds the chosen map nine times side by side with the same bricks, props, lamps, banners and mechanics (launch pads and teleporters work in every copy: each copy's animation runs with the fighters' positions shifted into that copy's own coordinates). Every seam gets two 8 m wide **roads** that run into each neighbour until they reach its main walkable area (found from a coarse 1 m picture of each copy's colliders), so every copy is reachable from every other, whatever the map has at its edges (moats, void, sealed walls). The walls and void boxes in the way are cut out of the colliders, the bricks in the way are removed and a brick-coloured road plate with studs is laid over water or void. - Things that used to assume a 60 m arena are now variables or recomputed: `A` in `game.js` is a `let` (30, or 90 in a match), `nav.js` recomputes its grid (and re-uses its A* arrays between searches with stamps, so the bigger grid does not make garbage), `STORM.size()` scales the red ground tint. CPU fighters start with 0 to 2 items so there is something to find.
